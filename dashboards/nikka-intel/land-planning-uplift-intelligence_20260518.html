⚠️ Planning Risk Checklist — Updated 18 May 2026

🔴 High Risk Factors

  • BFR data may be outdated. Brownfield registers are updated annually by councils — some entries may be 6-12 months old. A site marked "not permissioned" may now have PP granted. A "deliverable" site may have expired PP. Always verify individual sites on the relevant council planning portal before committing capital.
  • Council planning portals are increasingly JavaScript-rendered. Most modern council sites (Telford, Wyre Forest, Cannock Chase) use dynamic frontends that cannot be scraped via curl alone. This means BFR data must be supplemented with manual portal checks. Budget time for manual verification of top 10 priority sites.
  • Class MA eligibility requires 2+ years in Class E use. Not all commercial buildings qualify. The building must have been in Class E (or previously B1a) use for at least 2 years before the prior approval application. Buildings that have been vacant but not formally in E use may be ineligible. Always check the use class history.
  • Garage block contamination risk. Garage sites often have historical fuel/oil spills from vehicle maintenance. Ground contamination testing is essential before purchase commitment. Budget £2k-£5k for Phase 1 desktop study. If contamination found, remediation costs £10k-£50k depending on severity. Mitigation: clause in purchase contract for seller to indemnify historical contamination.
  • Bilston Urban Village Phase 2 may have condition satisfaction costs. Strategic sites often have pre-commencement conditions (archaeology, ecology, drainage, transport assessments) that add £5k-£25k in professional fees before development can start. Verify conditions in the planning decision notice.

🟡 Medium Risk Factors

  • BLRF Round 6 competition. £80M UK-wide for Round 6. WM estimated £12M-£18M. With 1,133 small sites in Birmingham alone, competition for grants will be intense. BLRF requires match funding and is competitive. Treat grant as bonus, not reliance, in financial models.
  • Kidderminster's DY10-DY11 house prices constrain margins. Lower quartile at £175k-£195k means garden infill and small developments need tight cost control. Build costs at £180k-£260k/unit leave little margin if land acquisition is not well-negotiated. Viability is sensitive to land price — don't overpay.
  • Walsall Lichfield Street sites — town centre market still uncertain. Walsall town centre has struggled with retail decline and empty units. While planning permission exists, end-user demand for new town centre apartments in Walsall is less certain than Birmingham/Solihull. Market demand assessment needed before committing.
  • Former Bean Cars site may need contamination remediation. As a former automotive industrial site (Bean Cars manufactured vehicles here historically), ground conditions may be poor. Phase 1 contamination survey essential. Budget £5k-£15k for remediation if needed.
  • B4/B5 Birmingham — HS2 uncertainty. The B4/B5 Jewellery Quarter/Digbeth corridor has been buoyed by HS2 Curzon Street expectations. With ongoing HS2 uncertainty (Manchester leg cancellation, budget overruns), the timeline and value impact of HS2 on this corridor is uncertain. Monitor HS2 progress statements before committing large capital.
