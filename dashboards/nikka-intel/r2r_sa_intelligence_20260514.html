🔥 NEW FINDINGS — Regulatory & Market Shifts NEW

1. Holiday Let Registration Scheme DELAYED to October 2026

The mandatory national short-term let register has been pushed back from April 2026 to October 2026. The Short Term Accommodation Association (STAA) CEO Andy Fenner confirmed: "While it would have been nice to have the register launched, it is probably more sensible now to do so in October."

Implication: Operators get a 6-month reprieve from registration obligations. But preparation should continue — the system is coming. Fines of £2,500 per property for non-compliance.

2. C5 Short-Term Let Use Class — Now Being Enforced

Properties let >90 nights/year now fall under C5 (short-term lets) use class. Changing from C3 (dwellinghouse) to C5 requires planning permission. Local authorities can adopt Article 4 Directions to remove permitted development rights. Already active in high-pressure areas.

Implication: Casual SA operators who exceed the 90-night threshold without planning permission are now operating illegally. This will reduce supply in tourist-heavy areas — but B2B mid-term operators using management partnerships (not leases) are structurally compliant.

3. Lenders Now Actively Competing for SA

Liquid Expat Mortgages CEO Stuart Marshall: "We are seeing a clear shift with specialist lenders not only accepting Serviced Accommodation, but actively competing to fund it." Up to 80% LTV available from specialist lenders. This is a structural shift — SA is no longer a "niche" financing product.

4. Savills Confirms Institutional Pivot to Serviced Apartments

Savills' European Serviced Apartment Report 2026 confirms: serviced apartments have 5.9% CAGR demand growth vs 1.1% hotel sector; 79% occupancy (vs 75% hotels); 86,911 rooms across 26 European gateway cities (only ~8% of total accommodation supply = massive under-penetration). Investor appetite +22%.

Key quote: "The sector benefits from a comparatively high margin, lower volatility operating model, underpinned by a bedroom-led cost structure and performance resilience."

5. "Between Houses" Airbnb Stays Now Legally Risky ⚠️ CRITICAL

Facebook UK Airbnb Hosts group confirms: "Stop offering 'between houses' stays after 1 May 2026 — These will almost always be classed as residential."

This is a direct consequence of RRA enforcement starting. If a guest uses the property as their main residence (receives post, registers with GP, stays more than 90 days), it becomes an assured tenancy. Landlord Law Blog (Feb 2026): "Finding tenants via Airbnb and calling yourself a 'host' and your tenants 'guests' does not exonerate you from the law!"

Implication for Kokal: This directly benefits compliant B2B mid-term operators who structure as licences (not tenancies) with service characteristics and limited duration. Non-compliant SA competition is being squeezed out.

6. Corporate Housing Market Confirmed at £2.8B, +34% YoY

Multiple sources converge: IBISWorld data, Savills, Eason Property, Housd. B2B mid-term corporate lets are the consensus winner for 2026. The Nottingham case study (Lawrence 2026) shows mid-term let at £25k-£30k gross revenue vs £12k AST on same property.

🔴 R2R Status — Death Confirmed

No new evidence contradicts the consensus. Foot Forward Properties (Feb 2026), Simon Zutshi (PIN), and multiple Reddit/YouTube sources all confirm: standard lease-based R2R is a dying model.
