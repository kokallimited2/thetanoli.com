🧠 Key Learning Points

1. The Ceiling Price Method (Purseglove) — Missing from Existing Frameworks

Instead of asking "is this property good at asking price?", Purseglove proposes:

  1. Define your minimum target cashflow (e.g. £350/month net for BTL)
  2. Fix your deposit / cash available (e.g. £75k max)
  3. Work BACKWARDS: at what purchase price does the deal hit your target?
  4. Compare Ceiling Price vs Asking Price — if ceiling < asking, the deal only works at a discount
🆕 This is a direct upgrade to our deal analysis workflow. Every analysis should include: "At [asking price]: £X/month. Ceiling price for £350 target: £Y. Gap: £Z."

3. "Buy As If Prices Will Never Rise" (Progressive Property)

Core philosophy: Cash flow priority over capital appreciation. Capital growth is a bonus, not a foundation. The 2001-2007 buyers learned this the hard way.

  • Base every deal on instant rental profitability
  • UMV creates instant equity + buffer against price drops
  • Remortgaging to remove deposit = 0% risk, infinite ROI
  • If a deal only works on capital growth assumptions → PASS

📈 Financial Models & Formulas

New Formulas to Add to Knowledge Base

Ceiling Purchase Price (BTL)
= (Target Monthly Cashflow + Monthly Mortgage + Monthly Costs) ÷ Monthly Rent Ratio
Where Monthly Costs = Management (10%) + Maintenance (5%) + Insurance + Voids (5%) + Service Charge
Section 24 Effective Tax Rate (Personal BTL)
Effective Tax = (Rental Income × Marginal Rate) − (Interest × 20%)
vs Limited Company: Effective Tax = (Rental Income − Interest) × Corporation Tax Rate

Rule of thumb: For higher-rate taxpayers, personal BTL carries ~2.8× the tax of Ltd Co BTL.
Leverage Multiplier (Total Return)
Total Return % = Rental Yield + (Capital Growth × Leverage Factor)
Leverage Factor = Property Value ÷ Cash Invested (e.g. 4× at 75% LTV)
Example: 2.3% yield + (3% growth × 4 leverage) = 2.3% + 12% = 14.3% total return
Capitalisation Rate (Cap Rate)
= Net Operating Income (NOI) ÷ Current Market Value × 100
UK analogue: Net Yield on market value, used for quick cross-property comparison
1% Rule (Quick Filter)
Monthly Rent ≥ Purchase Price × 0.01
Example: £100k property → minimum £1,000/month rent to pass quick filter.
Note: In current WM market, 1% rule is very aggressive (most achieve 0.5-0.7%). Use as aspirational benchmark, not rejection filter.

⚠️ Common Mistakes — Today's Additions

2026-05-15 — New Mistakes Identified
  • Not calculating a Ceiling Price — Evaluating a property only at asking price without working backwards from target cashflow. A property at £150k might yield £200/mo, but if your ceiling for £350/mo target is £130k, you need a discount. This is a different analysis than BRRR residual valuation.
  • Ignoring Section 24 in net cashflow projections — Many deal calculators show net profit before tax. For personal-name BTL at 40%+ marginal rate, the tax drag can turn a positive cashflow deal negative. Always model after-tax cashflow.
  • Over-killing the refurb — "Do not get drawn in by magical makeover TV programmes." Doing up a rental to your standard vs the market standard wastes capital that could go into the next deal. Kitchen + bathroom = highest ROI, everything else = diminishing returns.
  • No maintenance fund pre-commitment — "Build the maintenance fund before you buy the property, not after something breaks." Set aside 10% of monthly rent into a separate savings account from day one.
  • Buying without a clear exit strategy — "Only thinking about the purchase limits flexibility when tax rules or market conditions shift." Every deal needs: primary exit, secondary exit, tertiary exit — stress tested.
  • Not building the right team — DIY on accounting, legal, sourcing, and finance slows you down and increases risk. Surround yourself with specialists who understand property investment.

⚡ Today's Improvement

🆕 Add "Ceiling Price" Step to Every BTL Deal Analysis

Problem: The current deal analysis framework evaluates a property at its asking price and reports what yield/cashflow it produces. This answers "is this deal good?" but not "what should I pay to make this deal good?"

Upgrade: Every BTL deal analysis must now include a Ceiling Price Calculation — a reverse calculation that determines the maximum purchase price required to hit a target net cashflow.

  1. Define target: Minimum net monthly cashflow (e.g. £300/mo for West Midlands BTL, higher for HMO)
  2. Calculate monthly costs: Mortgage + Management (10%) + Maintenance (5%) + Insurance + Voids (5%) + Service Charge/Ground Rent
  3. Derive required monthly net rent: Target Cashflow + Total Monthly Costs
  4. Determine required purchase price: Set up a sensitivity table showing cashflow at different price points, or use the ratio method
  5. Compare Ceiling Price vs Asking Price:
    • If asking ≤ ceiling → deal passes on price (other factors still need checking)
    • If asking > ceiling by <10% → negotiate hard (discount needed)
    • If asking > ceiling by ≥10% → PASS (mispriced for your strategy)
  6. Include in Deal Card: Add a "Ceiling Price" row showing the max bid and the gap to asking

Why this matters: The Ceiling Price method transforms analysis from passive ("what does this property return?") to active ("what price makes this property work for me?"). It forces the investor to define their minimum acceptable outcome before negotiating, preventing the most common mistake: accepting a price that looks reasonable but doesn't actually meet their targets.
