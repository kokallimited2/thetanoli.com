💡 Today's Planning Insight

The 2025/26 Planning Reform Window is the single biggest opportunity for West Midlands planning uplift since the 2012 NPPF changes.

  • Planning & Infrastructure Bill 2025 — Compulsory Purchase reforms lowering land costs, local planning fees, presumption-to-permit for councils delivering <75% housing target
  • NPPF changes (Dec 2024) — Green Belt development no longer "inappropriate" where councils lack 5-year supply. This is a GAME CHANGER for stalled sites ringing Dudley, Walsall, Wolverhampton
  • Brownfield First — West Midlands has 190,000+ homes of brownfield capacity. 60% already have PP. The remaining 40% (~76,000 homes) are the opportunity.
  • Class MA Permitted Development — Commercial-to-residential conversions at record highs. Vacancy requirement only 3 months. No full planning needed.
  • Black Country Plan delays — With the BCP stalled, councils are individually adopting local plans. Creates a window where sites can be promoted ahead of formal allocation.

Key takeaway: The best uplift strategy right now is acquiring non-permissioned brownfield sites in the Black Country, securing outline planning via the presumption-to-permit, and selling to volume housebuilders.

🔧 Improved Hermes Rules

1. Brownfield Register First, Search Later. Before searching portals, check planning.data.gov.uk for the council's brownfield register. These sites are pre-vetted as suitable for housing. Focus on "Not Permissioned" sites — that's where the planning uplift opportunity is.
2. Cross-Reference Every Planning Status. For any site found via plot portals (PlotBrowser, PlotFinder), verify the actual PP status on the council planning portal. Plot portals may list expired or lapsed permissions.
3. Check HDT Results Before Assessing Green Belt Sites. Councils delivering <75% of housing target are under presumption-to-permit. In the West Midlands, identify which councils fail this test — Green Belt sites near those councils have dramatically higher PP likelihood.
4. Three-Tier Scoring. Tier 1 (≥8.0) = Full report and Telegram alert. Tier 2 (7.0-7.9) = Watchlist, needs manual verification. Tier 3 (<7.0) = Store for reference, no active pursuit.
5. Always Model the 'Without Planning' Baseline. Uplift is only real if you can acquire at unimproved value. For every opportunity, establish: (a) current use value, (b) market value of land without PP, (c) value with PP. The spread between (b) and (c) is the true uplift opportunity.
6. Auction Lots Are the Fastest Route. Bond Wolfe and Town & Country Property Auctions consistently list West Midlands land at guide prices 30-70% below GDV. Monitor auction calendars weekly. Pre-register to bid.
7. Class MA Conversions Deserve Their Own Search. Don't just look for "land" — search for vacant Commercial (Class E) units, former pubs (Sui Generis), offices, and banks in town centre locations. These can convert to residential via PD without full planning.
8. Build a Local Authority-Specific Playbook. Each West Midlands council has different planning appetites. Early data suggests: Dudley = pro-small-scale, Sandwell = pro-brownfield regeneration, Wolverhampton = mixed (pro-density in centre, cautious in suburbs), Walsall = brownfield-led, Telford = large-scale growth area.
9. Always Check Auction Results After Each Auction Day. Lots that didn't sell at auction may accept post-auction offers. Turls Hill Road, Coseley (guide £50k) — if unsold yesterday, approach Bond Wolfe for a post-auction offer.
10. Track Lapsed Permissions Register. Sites like Douglass Road, Dudley (£120k, lapsed PP) are effectively pre-de-risked — the council already approved the principle of development. Renewing lapsed PP is cheaper, faster, and higher success rate than fresh applications.
