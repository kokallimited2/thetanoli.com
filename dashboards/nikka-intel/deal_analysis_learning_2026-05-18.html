🧠 Structured Deal Analysis Framework (Updated — Synthesis)

Step 1 — Define Investment Strategy First
Before touching numbers, define which strategy this property is being analysed for. Common strategies: BTL, BRRR, Flip, HMO, SA, Commercial Conversion. Each has different success metrics. The property must be evaluated against a strategy, not in isolation.

Step 2 — Establish True Market Value (Comparable Evidence)
Use recent completed sales of similar properties (type, size, condition, micro-location). Reject estate agent opinions and online valuation tools as primary sources. Apply evidence gap penalties for condition, EPC, or structural differences.

Step 3 — Assess Rental Demand & Achievable Income
Use actual let-agreed prices, not advertised rents. Consider tenant demand depth, property condition/specification, local affordability constraints, and void assumptions. Overestimating rent is the fastest way to destroy viability.

Step 4 — Full Cost Waterfall
Include ALL costs: SDLT + survey + valuation + solicitor + broker + sourcing fees + refurb + contingency (15-20%) + finance (bridging interest, arrangement fees, interest during voids) + holding costs + exit costs (agent fees, CGT, selling legal).

Step 5 — Three-Layer Profitability
Show Gross Yield, Net Yield, and Cash-on-Cash ROI. Headline yields are misleading. Also model IRR for cross-property comparison. Returns must be assessed relative to risk and capital deployed.

Step 6 — Sensitivity & Scenario Analysis
Test under multiple scenarios: conservative, base, optimistic. Identify break-even points, exposure to interest rate changes, and margin of safety. A deal that only works under perfect conditions is rarely a good deal.

Step 7 — Risk Assessment
Market risk (price volatility, liquidity), regulatory risk (licensing, EPC, planning), execution risk (contractor delays, cost overruns, poor project management), and refinance risk (lender criteria tightening).

Step 8 — Structured Comparison
Use standardised assumptions and framework-driven analysis rather than intuition or ad-hoc spreadsheets. This enables objective comparison between deals and eliminates decision inconsistency.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Using asking prices instead of sold evidence — Asking price is a negotiation start point, not market value. Check actual Land Registry/comparable sold data before committing to a valuation.
  • Overestimating rental income — Advertised rents are aspirational. Use let-agreed evidence only. Overestimating rent by even 10% can flip a deal from positive to negative cashflow.
  • Ignoring total finance costs — Many calculators include mortgage interest but not arrangement fees, exit fees, valuation fees, or interest during refurb/void periods. These can add 2-4% to total project cost.
  • Failing to stress-test assumptions — A deal that only works under perfect conditions (full occupancy, no rate rises, on-budget refurb) is not a deal at all. Test at -10% rent, +2% rates, +15% costs.
  • Analysing deals without a clear strategy — Without a defined strategy (BTL vs BRRR vs flip), you don't know what metric matters. A 7% yield property with no refurb scope might be perfect for BTL but useless for BRRR.
  • Not planning for the 2026-27 refinance cycle — 1.8M UK mortgages due to reset by end of 2027. If your deal depends on fixed-rate mortgages taken out in 2021-22, model what happens when they reset to current rates.

🎯 Application

Today's immediate application: When evaluating any BRRR deal, always model what happens to cashflow after the initial bridging loan is replaced by a BTL mortgage at current (2026) rates. Many BRRR calculators only show the "equity released" figure without modelling whether the post-refi monthly cashflow is sustainable. A deal that releases £50k equity but produces -£100/mo cashflow after refinancing is not viable — it forces either a distressed sale or constant cash injections.

Practical rule: For every BRRR analysis, add a row to the Deal Card titled "Post-Refi Cashflow at Current Rates" showing the monthly position after refinance. Flag in red if negative.
