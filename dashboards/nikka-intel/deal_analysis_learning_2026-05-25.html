📊 Key Learning Points

🟢 1. Holding Costs Are the "Silent Profit Killer"

RefurbCalculator explicitly quantifies: a typical 6-month refurb with bridging finance incurs £6,000-£10,000 in holding costs alone (bridging interest at 0.7-1%/mo, unoccupied insurance, council tax, utilities). This is money that comes directly off profit and is routinely underestimated by first-time developers. Most frameworks include holding costs in theory but under-budget them by 50%+.

🟢 2. Room-by-Room Refurb Estimation Is the ONLY Reliable Method

Cost-per-sqm is explicitly called out as "getting developers into trouble" — a blanket £1,500/sqm tells you nothing about whether the property needs a full rewire, new kitchen, or just decoration. The only reliable approach is a room-by-room estimate based on what you actually see at the property, using current regional labour and material rates. This validates our use of the Refurb Calculator tool and the "requires modernisation" vs "recently refurbished" distinction.

🟢 3. The 70% Rule Is a Starting Point, Not a Decision

The US-originated 70% rule (max offer = (ARV × 0.70) − refurb costs) has structural limits in the UK market. The SDLT surcharge (+5% on second properties from Apr 2025) and bridging finance costs (£3,000-8,000) add 8-15% that the base formula doesn't capture. A work-backwards-from-target-profit method produces a genuinely reliable number: Target profit + refurb + SDLT + finance + legal + agent fees = max costs. ARV − max costs = max offer. This is the residual method we already use, but the 70% rule can be calibrated as a quick visual filter (not a decision tool).

🟢 4. Return on Equity (ROE) Is the BRRR Metric That Matters

Gross yield and net yield against end value are secondary metrics for BRRR. The primary metric is Return on Equity: Annual Net Income ÷ Cash Left In Deal. A clean refinance (Money Left In = £0) produces mathematically infinite ROE. Every incremental £5k left in reduces ROE by ~2-4%. This validates and reinforces our existing Money Left In Framework (Improvement #6, 2026-05-19).

🟢 5. Exit Costs Drive the BRRR vs Flip Decision

The RefurbCalculator's worked example reveals the hidden cost of flipping: £152,525 total costs on a £175k sale (including SDLT £6,800, agent fees £2,625+VAT, 6mo bridging £5,400) = only £22,475 profit (12.8% margin). BRRR on the same numbers: £131,250 refi covers 97.2% of costs, leaving just £3,750 in the deal. The flip's "return-all-capital" advantage is substantially eroded by selling costs — BRRR preserves more capital for the next deal.

🧠 Best Deal Analysis Framework (Updated)

Phase A — Pre-Screen (Under 30 Seconds)

  1. Stage 0a — Institutional 5-Filter (Price ≤ 75% ARV, Price-to-rent <100×, Net yield ≥6%, Let-agreed ≥35%, Triple-exit optionality)
  2. Stage 0b — Two-Loan Alignment Check (Pre-qualify refinance exit, assess 6-month rule, stress proceeds at 85% of standard underwriting)
  3. Stage 0c — Blind Spot Audit (Quantify input uncertainty ranges, grade source quality A/B/C, define exit criteria before modelling, flag blind confidence)
  4. Stage 0 — 4-Factor Triage (Price-fit 2.5pts, Refurb-signal 2.5pts, Area-demand 2.5pts, Quick-yield 2.5pts. Pass ≥ 6/10)

Phase B — Detailed Analysis (20-Minute Session)

  1. Step 1: Establish End Value (GDV) — 3+ comparable sold prices, ¼ mile, same type/size, last 12 months. Be conservative: if comps range £170-190k, use £175k not £190k. Apply Dual-Factor Valuation Gap (Standard × ESG).
  2. Step 2: Estimate Refurb Cost — Room-by-room based on what you see. NOT cost-per-sqm (it's unreliable and gets developers into trouble). Add HMO compliance costs (£3k-£6k for 6-bed) if applicable. Contingency: 15-20%.
  3. Step 3: Calculate Total Acquisition Costs — Purchase price + SDLT (5% surcharge on second properties from Apr 2025) + legal (£1k-£2k) + survey + bridging arrangement fee (1-2%) + valuation fee.
  4. Step 4: Calculate Holding Costs — Bridging interest (0.7-1%/mo = £700-1,000/mo per £100k), unoccupied insurance, council tax, utilities. Typical total for 6-month refurb: £6,000-10,000. These are the "silent profit killer."
  5. Step 5: Calculate Exit Costs — Flip: agent fees (1-1.5% + VAT) + selling legal + CGT/trading income tax. BRRR: refinance arrangement + valuation + legal fees. No agent fees or immediate tax event for BRRR.
  6. Step 6: Run the Numbers at Asking Price — If deal stacks with conservative assumptions → make offer. If not → calculate the price where it does stack and negotiate. If gap too wide → walk away.

Phase C — The Work-Backwards Method (Most Reliable)

Work backwards from your target profit:
Target profit + Refurb costs + SDLT + Finance costs + Buying legal + Selling legal + Agent fees = Total non-purchase costs
ARV − Total non-purchase costs = Maximum Offer Price
This is more work than the 70% formula, but gives a number you can rely on.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

(NEW) 1. Treating the 70% Rule as Exact Instead of Calibrating for UK Costs

The 70% rule originated in US flipping where transaction costs are lower. UK costs — 5% SDLT surcharge, 0.7-1%/mo bridging interest, 1.5%+VAT agent fees — add 8-15% to the spread that the formula doesn't account for. Always run the full work-backwards calculation before making an offer, not just the 70% formula.

(NEW) 2. Underestimating Holding Costs by 50%+

RefurbCalculator quantifies: 6 months of holding on a typical £100k bridging loan = £4,200-6,000 in interest alone, plus insurance (£100-200/mo for unoccupied), council tax (£100-200/mo), and utilities (£50-100/mo). Total: £6,000-10,000. Most first-time developers budget £2,000-3,000 and get caught out by the gap.

(NEW) 3. Using Cost-per-sqm Refurb Estimates

Blanket £/sqm rates cannot distinguish between a property needing decoration vs full rewire + new heating + structural work. This is called out as "getting developers into trouble." The only reliable method is room-by-room estimation based on what you actually see. This validates why we use RefurbCalculator's tool.

(NEW) 4. Flipping Without Accounting for Trading Income Tax

HMRC may treat flip profits as trading income (not CGT) — meaning income tax rates up to 45%, not the lower CGT rates. This catches first-time flippers off guard. A £22.5k flip profit at 40% income tax = £9k to HMRC, reducing net to £13.5k. This is embedded in the RefurbCalculator's BRRR vs Flip analysis and is a critical differentiator.

(NEW) 5. Comparing BRRR and Flip on Profit Alone

BRRR and flip are structurally different investments — one gives one-off cash (flip), the other gives ongoing income + asset growth (BRRR). The correct comparison is over a 2-5 year horizon, not at exit. A flip reinvested two more times may outperform a single BRRR, but BRRR compounds more efficiently for portfolio builders.
