🔑 #1 — The Two-Loan Sequence Paradigm (BRRR is Not a Single Loan)

"Most BRRRR deals don't fail at purchase — they fail at refinance. BRRRR isn't a single loan. It's a two-loan sequence and the two need to be aligned from day one." — HomeAbroad

The fundamental paradigm shift: BRRR is not a single transaction with a single financing source. It is a two-loan sequence:

  1. Loan 1: Bridging / fix-and-flip loan (acquisition + refurb)
  2. Loan 2: BTL / DSCR mortgage (refinance + hold)

The critical mistake: Investors close on Loan 1 without pre-qualifying what Loan 2 will actually require. Result: 3+ months over timeline, paying bridging interest, refinance clock hasn't even started.

Fix: Get a DSCR/BTL underwriter to pre-qualify the refinance exit before closing on bridging. Lender who does both products = seamless transition.

    ⚠️ Mistake #1: Treating BRRR as a single loan transaction

    BRRR is a two-loan sequence — bridging + BTL. Most investors analyse the purchase and refurb but never pre-qualify the refinance exit. Result: the refinance doesn't happen on the modelled timeline or at the modelled LTV. The two loans must be aligned from day one, ideally with the same lender handling both products.

    Source: HomeAbroad (500+ BRRRR deals structured)

    ⚠️ Mistake #2: Modelling refinance proceeds at 2022-era underwriting levels

    Leverage has collapsed 25% from 2022 to 2025 ($12.16→$8.88 debt per $1 NOI). Many 2025/2026 deal spreadsheets still use 2022 underwriting assumptions for refinance modelling. The same property produces 25% less capital on refinance today. If you're not stress-testing at reduced proceeds, you're modelling a fantasy.

    Sources: Kendall Norfork / HomeAbroad, Adam Lawrence

    ⚠️ Mistake #3: Treating refurb overrun and surveyor gap as independent risks

    They compound non-linearly. A 15% refurb overrun + 7.5% surveyor gap doesn't create a 22.5% capital deficit — it creates a 50-80% larger gap because the overrun increases the all-in cost and the surveyor gap reduces the mortgage proceeds on that higher cost base. The Property Filter real worked example shows £24k projected → £39k actual = 62.5% worse than projected.

    Source: Property Filter — real worked example

    ⚠️ Mistake #4: Assuming the 6-month rule applies equally to all lenders

    Mainstream BTL lenders (BM Solutions, The Mortgage Works, Paragon) enforce 6 months from Land Registry registration. With backlogs, that's 8-9 months of bridging. But specialist lenders with manual underwriting can refinance within weeks of completion if the refurb is substantial and well-documented. Most investors don't know this workaround exists and accept 8-9 months of bridging as inevitable.

    Source: Adam Lawrence — "UK BRRR Strategy 2026"

    ⚠️ Mistake #5: Using gross yield as a primary screening metric

    DealSheetAI's Birmingham case study proves that a 6.6% gross yield can deliver just 1.28% net. The UK average gap between gross and net is 28% of gross. For WM with higher management and void costs, assume a minimum 50% haircut from gross to net. If gross yield is 7%, expect 3.5% net or lower. Screen on net yield, not gross.

    Source: DealSheetAI — rent yield calculator guide
