📊 Market Overview

The Renters' Rights Act has now been live for one full month. The market impact is accelerating: casual R2R operators are exiting in droves, leisure SA occupancy has collapsed to pre-pandemic lows, and the only growth segment is B2B mid-term corporate housing (1–6 months, average stay ~83 days).

Key data points this week:

  • Corporate housing market: £2.8 billion, surging 34% year-on-year (LinkedIn / industry data). IBISWorld confirms industry revenue at £1.5B with 12.4% growth trajectory.
  • Leisure SA occupancy crash: Forward pacing for April 2026 now at 29%, March at 32%, February at 37%. Break-even is 35–45%. Most leisure operators are now losing money for 6+ months of the year.
  • Platform fee squeeze: Airbnb's 15.5% host-only fee (up from legacy 3%) now extracts ~18.3% effective revenue when VAT is included. Booking.com commissions run 15–25%.
  • Reddit operator sentiment: Multiple hosts reporting 85% booking drops since the October 2025 algorithm change. Oversaturation is the #1 cited cause. "Bad hosting has turned a lot of guests off" — market fatigue is real.
  • Standard R2R: Definitively dead. All sub-tenancies are now Periodic Assured Tenancies. No Section 21. Head landlords jointly liable for licensing offences. Civil penalties up to £7,000 for fixed-term clauses and £40,000 for serious breaches.

Consensus verdict: The "missing middle" (mid-term corporate lets) is the only viable arbitrage left. It eliminates platform fees, seasonality, and high turnover costs while capturing hospitality-level pricing with near-residential stability.

  • HIGH — Standard lease-based R2R. Section 21 abolished. All tenancies periodic. Head landlords jointly liable. Most BTL mortgages prohibit sub-letting. One bad tenant wipes out a year's profit.

  • LOW — Management partnership disputes. Mitigate with clear written agreements, 30-day termination clauses, and transparent accounting.

🧠 Today's Edge

The 83-day mid-term corporate let is the single most important arbitrage in UK property right now. It is not a theory — it is a mathematical fact: by eliminating OTA commissions (15–20%), high-turnover housekeeping (up to 45% of gross in leisure SA), and seasonality volatility, the mid-term model compresses operating costs from 43% of revenue down to 12–17%. Meanwhile, B2B clients (insurers, corporates, relocation agents) pay 20–30% above AST rates for the convenience of flexible, furnished, all-inclusive housing. The result is a 15%+ net yield with lower risk than either traditional BTL or leisure SA.

The critical insight: This is not about finding "corporate tenants on Airbnb." That is a leisure strategy with business branding. True corporate housing is B2B: direct contracts with loss adjusters (ClaimTrak, ICAB), relocation agents, and HR departments. The operator who builds these relationships first captures a recurring revenue stream that no leisure competitor can touch. Start local — Dudley/Birmingham corridor — then scale the playbook nationally.
