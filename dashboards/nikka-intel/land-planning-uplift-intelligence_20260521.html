🧠 New Lessons Learned

1. Birmingham City Council garage/land sites: 20+ opportunities available in 2026. NEW

Birmingham City Council is proposing freehold disposal of 20+ former garage sites and small land parcels in 2026 (Save Birmingham, via Cabinet Property Committee Feb/Mar 2026). Locations confirmed include Four Oaks Common Road (£50k+), Howard Road East Kings Heath (618 sqm), and Hazeldene Road Sheldon. These typically sell for £10k-£50k at auction and can achieve 200-500% uplift with planning. This is the most significant pipeline of low-entry-cost development land identified to date. Approach: target these via Bond Wolfe auctions, check planning potential individually, expect stiff competition (Birmingham land at 340% premium on recent auction).

2. Former M&S Wolverhampton→71 flats sets city centre conversion benchmark. NEW

ALB Group (Nottingham) purchased the former M&S at Dudley Street, Wolverhampton for £3m+ in 2025. Planning APPROVED (May 2026) for 71 one-and-two-bed flats across 4 floors, with retail retained on ground floor. Building is adjacent to but outside the conservation area. This is the largest single commercial→resi conversion in Wolverhampton in recent years. It sets a powerful precedent for other large-format commercial buildings in Wolverhampton city centre, especially within the Canalside South halo zone (first completions June 2026).

3. Walsall Grey Belt appeals developer-friendly — 115 homes won at Longwood Lane. NEW

William Davis Homes Ltd won a planning appeal for 115 homes on Green Belt at Longwood Lane, Walsall (Express & Star, Feb 2026). The inspector accepted the grey belt argument — the site is previously developed, bounded by existing development, and doesn't strongly contribute to Green Belt purposes. With Walsall maintaining <2 year housing land supply, developers are consistently winning at appeal. 7,000+ homes proposed in the draft Local Plan including 500 at Bosty Lane. Strategy: target Grey Belt edge-of-Walsall sites with low land costs (£20k-£80k/acre for agricultural) and factor in 50% affordable requirement under Golden Rules.

4. Wolverhampton pub-to-resi pipeline active — two case studies confirmed. NEW

(a) Bagot Arms, Whitmore Reans — closed 2008 after fatal shooting, derelict 18 years. 0.47 acre, PP for 8 retail + 3 flats (2023). Sold to local developer Mar 2026. Towler Shaw Roberts handled. (b) Happy Wanderer, Bilston — closed 2017, demolished 2021. Now 19 affordable homes by Keon Homes + Black Country Housing Group, grant from Homes England. Both sites show Wolverhampton Council supports pub→resi conversion, especially via empty properties taskforce. Target former pubs with existing PP or in residential areas.

5. Telford PIP garden/infill route now has dual validation — two approvals in May 2026. NEW

(a) Spring Terrace, The Rock — PIP for up to 3 homes (TWC/2026/0292), Mid-West Planning Ltd agent. Previous PP expired 2008. (b) Summerhill, Sutton Hill — Fred Chambers, PIP for 1 home on 113 sqm infill plot APPROVED despite 8 objections. No vehicle access — relies on existing parking. Must prove 10% BNG. Both applications handled by Midwest Planning Ltd, showing a dedicated Telford agent has cracked the PIP garden formula. Replicable model for any similar corner/road-end garden plot in Telford & Wrekin.

6. Bellway 74 homes at Hednesford — Cannock development corridor validated. NEW

Bellway West Midlands received detailed PP for 74 homes at Green Oaks, Hednesford (6 acres, part of 900-home wider development). £500k school contribution, £15k bus shelter, £7.5k cycle storage. First homes released for sale. Sales office opening August 2026. This validates Cannock as an active development corridor with major housebuilders committed. Adjacent infill opportunities likely to emerge as the wider 900-home scheme progresses.

7. £1m WMCA investment restarts stalled Walsall scheme — 71 social rent homes. NEW

WMCA invested £1m from Mayor Richard Parker's regeneration funds to restart a stalled housing scheme in Walsall. 71 social rent homes by whg housing association, completion spring 2027. Shows WMCA is actively deploying capital to bail out stalled housing schemes in the target area, indicating strong political will to deliver housing regardless of developer difficulties.

8. Land investment in 2026: 10-100x uplift potential but 10-30% success rate. NEW

BuyLand.co.uk 2026 guide confirms: agricultural→residential planning = 10-100x value increase. But only 10-30% of strategic land applications succeed. Realistic: land with PP achieves 15-30% profit margin over 1-3 years. Self-build development: 20-40% of final property value over 2-4 years. Key lesson: never pay "hope value" — always price land assuming no planning, and treat planning gain as the bonus.
