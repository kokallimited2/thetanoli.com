🧠 New Lessons Learned

1. Garage/garage-block sites are the highest-ROI overlooked asset class in the West Midlands.

Former garage sites (municipal or private) sell at £25k-£50k, often with historic planning permissions for 2-4 dwellings. They sit in established residential areas, have existing access roads, and are unlikely to face Green Belt/conservation constraints. The Council is often the motivated seller (Sandwell, Dudley councils actively selling surplus garage sites).

2. "Garden grabbing" policies vary drastically between councils — but small infill works.

Dudley Council rejected a single 2-bed home as "garden grabbing" in Stourbridge (Amblecote), yet approved 5 new homes on Bath Street, Sedgley and 6 homes on Mill Garage, Kingswinford. The difference: former commercial/garage sites get approval; rear garden plots in conservation-sensitive areas don't. Pre-application advice is essential.

3. Pub-to-residential conversions are Dudley Council's preferred conversion type.

Red Lion, Brierley Hill (ground floor to 2 flats approved). Former Cock Inn, Rowley Regis (planning for 3 houses OR 9 flats). Old Chain Yard, Coseley (rejected for convenience store, but pub itself has heritage status). Target former pubs with existing upper-floor flats — the conversion precedent is already set.

4. Class MA (commercial-to-residential) is a faster route than full planning.

Permitted Development Rights for commercial-to-residential conversion (Class MA) bypass full planning for change of use. Especially valuable for mixed-use high street properties in Bilston, Brierley Hill, Halesowen, Dudley town centre. Non-residential SDLT rates (max 5%) make these significantly cheaper than pure residential purchases.

5. Ancient woodland is a deal-killer — never assume "development potential" means buildable.

The Turls Hill Road, Coseley 11.5-acre plot sold for £90,000 at Bond Wolfe's 50th auction, but was identified as 750-year-old ancient woodland with fears it could "be targeted by builders." National planning policy gives ancient woodland the highest protection level. The £90k price reflects the buyer likely bought it for its current use (woodland/amenity), not development.

6. Planning uplift without building (sell with planning permission) is the safest strategy.

Planning By Design (April 2026 article) confirmed that securing planning and selling the site captures the majority of uplift without construction risk. Example: Land bought at £25k without planning → planning for 1-2 dwellings obtained → sold for £75k-£150k. 200-500% return with zero build risk.
