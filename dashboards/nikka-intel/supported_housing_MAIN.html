📋 Key Findings

1. Ash-Shahada exits Birmingham (BREAKING — 17 May 2026) — One of the largest SEA providers, under RSH investigation for 5 years, is quitting Birmingham entirely. Up to 3,000 tenants across hundreds of properties will be transitioned to new providers. The board cited "regulatory exposure associated with lease-based supported housing models" as the reason — a direct admission that the lease-based model faces existential risk. CEO Toaha Qureshi and deputy CE Umar Mahmood face High Court allegations of financial impropriety. The firm claimed £25.5M in enhanced HB in 2025 alone. This is the single biggest destabilisation event in the UK SEA sector since the 2023 Act.

2. Lease-based provider model under regulatory siege — Portus (merged lease-based RP, 2,300 homes) assigned non-compliant interim grades from day one (8 May 2026). Pivotal HA faces compulsory deregistration. A 26,000-home Birmingham HA under investigation for "serious failings" (13 May 2026). The RSH is systematically targeting the lease-based model — "merge out of trouble" is no longer viable.

3. LHA freeze confirmed for 2026-27 — Rates frozen at April 2024 levels per SI 2026/5. This is the third consecutive year of frozen LHA. Rising rents + frozen HB = pressure on SEA margins. However, for Kokal's model (freehold + RP lease), the freeze is a low direct risk because the RP manages HB claims. CPI-indexed leases remain the safest hedge.

4. Supported living tenders in 2026 — Outcome-based commissioning is now standard. Workforce quality and retention is the #1 scoring factor. Social value accounts for 15-25% of total bid score. Housing/care separation is mandatory (CQC scrutiny). Critical rule: NEVER buy property before winning the tender — Kokal's RP partnership model mitigates this.

5. SAHP 2026-2036 & Rent Standard provide policy certainty — £27.3B SAHP confirmed with £1.2B bridge funding. For-profit RPs eligible. National Housing Bank opening £1B in low-cost loans Autumn 2026. L&G scalable partnership model could unlock £9.2B/year. Rent Standard 2026: CPI+1% for 10 years — unprecedented revenue predictability for social housing.

6. Provider due diligence checklist established — 8-point framework for vetting RP partners: RSH grading (G1/V1 minimum), asset backing (avoid lease-only), 5-year track record, framework status (Dudley MBC), CQC registration, tenant outcomes, HB dependency ratio, and concentration risk.

🏘️ 2026-05-15 — Licensing Detail, Council-By-Council Analysis, Property Specs & Provider Landscape

📊 Key Stats

  • HQN published detailed licensing guidance (May 2026) — scope, exemptions, FPPT, staged enforcement
  • Birmingham SEAQS preferred provider list: 3 Gold/Silver awards published (BVSC)
  • £18.2M allocated to West Midlands for supported housing (2026–29), mostly Birmingham + Coventry
  • 6–12% supported housing yields vs 4.8% standard BTL
  • 34% shortfall in suitable units nationally (NHF)
  • Property adaptation costs: £8k–£25k per property
  • 23% year-on-year SEA demand growth

📋 Key Findings

1. Licensing regime final shape (HQN, May 2026) — Detailed guidance confirms: LA-level licences (not per-scheme), 5-year duration, risk-based discretionary inspection, staged enforcement (notice → 3-month improvement → penalty → revocation), enhanced FPPT including Service Manager condition. Discretionary conditions dropped entirely — national conditions only. Expanded exemptions for commissioned services, Ofsted-regulated beds, 55+ regulated housing, and domestic abuse services.

2. West Midlands council analysis completed — Dudley (9/10) is the best entry point: no additional HMO licensing for 3-4 bed properties, low SEA saturation, home base. Walsall (8/10) has zero Article 4 restrictions and no additional HMO licensing — strong Phase 2 target. Sandwell (7/10) has borough-wide additional licensing but low oversaturation. Birmingham (3/10) is severely oversaturated (32,000 residents, 11,200 properties, enforcement capacity collapsed).

3. Preferred providers identified — Spring Housing (developed SEA Charter of Rights, Birmingham partner), Midland Mencap (learning disability specialist, WM presence, good for Dudley entry), YMCA Birmingham (institutional credibility, under-25 specialist), Golden Lane Housing (national RP, learning disability, high standards). BVSC SEAQS assessment is the gold standard — will likely mirror SHROA 2027 licensing.

4. Property specifications documented — Minimum: 2-3 bed semi/terraced freehold, EPC D+ (C preferred), 35+ sqm for single occupancy, standard construction, working CH, fire safety to Grade D LD2 minimum. Ideal: 50+ sqm, EPC C+, natural light, gas combi, pre-wired for assistive tech. Modifications: wet room (£3k–£5k), widened doors (£500–£1,500/door), fire safety (£2k–£5k), stairlift (£2k–£4k), assistive tech (£1k–£4k). Total £8k–£25k depending on property. Payback: 2–3 years.

5. Financial models benchmarked — Single-occupancy lease: 6–8% net yield, 20–28% ROCE, zero voids. HMO model: 30–40% above standard HMO yields but needs Article 4 check (WAIVED in Dudley/Walsall). Commercial conversion: 10–12% gross (Class MA, lower SDLT at commercial rates). Worked example at £130k purchase = 9.6% net yield, 28.3% ROCE.

6. Stress test passed — Property survives 14-week void or 35% rent cut before yield drops below BTL baseline (4.8%). Breaker point well within manageable range. Recommend 6-month rent reserve per property.

7. Pre-licensing window identified — Local Supported Housing Strategies due March 2027. Licensing goes live sometime 2027. Kokal has a ~10-month window to establish quality partnerships and purchase properties before the full regulatory framework formalises. Act now.

  • Phase 2 (2027): 4–6 properties across Dudley + Walsall + Sandwell, add 1 commercial conversion, SHROA licence held by RP per LA area
  • Phase 3 (2028+): Review Birmingham entry post-SHROA clearance. Consider purpose-built 8–12 unit scheme.

🏘️ 2026-05-13 — Regulatory Earthquake: Government Response, Birmingham Crisis, Licensing Details & Kokal Entry Strategy

📊 Key Stats

  • Birmingham SEA annual spend: £400M (half of England's total)
  • 33,000 people in Birmingham SEA across 11,200 properties — tripled in 8 years
  • 50,000+ supported homes at risk of closure nationally (NHF)
  • 581 consultation responses to the Supported Housing (Regulatory Oversight) Act
  • Licensing regime confirmed for 2027 (from some time)
  • SEA investment yields: 6–12% depending on model
  • Projected UK need: 1–1.3M units by 2040

📋 Key Findings

1. Government response published 16 April 2026 — major concessions won by the sector. Key win: scheme-level licensing dropped in favour of LA-level provider licences listing all addresses. Discretionary conditions scrapped — national conditions only. Definitions of "care/support/supervision" paused. Planning use class not introduced (review in 3 years). But no funding for compliance costs, HB subsidy loss unaddressed.

2. Birmingham crisis intensifies — Westminster Hall debate (Feb 2026) revealed only 42% of SEA properties needed for local demand; 58% import residents from other LAs. Organised crime, money laundering, and drug dealing linked to rogue providers. The council's multi-agency team (21 staff, recovered £8.8M, 2,600 ASB investigations) runs out of funding March 2026 — government not renewing.

3. HQ Network published licensing guidance (May 2026) — risk-based discretionary inspection, staged enforcement (notice → improvement → enforcement), expanded exemption categories.

4. HMO licensing STILL applies to SEA — Sandwell Council confirms: "There is no national rule saying supported or exempt accommodation does not require an HMO licence." This is critical for Kokal's property acquisition strategy.

5. Lease-based provider model flagged as high risk — RSH has been warning since First Priority HA (2018). 40% void losses documented, thin capitalisation, poor governance. Newly merged lease-based providers receiving non-compliant grades. Pivotal HA facing compulsory deregistration.

🎯 Kokal Recommended Strategy

  • AVOID Birmingham SEA — 50% oversupply, enforcement capacity collapsing, organised crime links
  • START in Dudley (DY5/DY8) — home base, lower saturation, early-mover advantage
  • Phase 1 (now–Dec 2026): 1–2 single-occupancy supported living flats with guaranteed rent to established RP (Spring Housing, Midland Mencap, YMCA). Target 6–8% yield. Modify (£8k–£25k/property). Budget ~£400k for 2 properties
  • Phase 2 (2027): Scale to 4–6 properties across Dudley + Sandwell + Walsall. Apply SHROA licence. Add 1 commercial conversion
  • Phase 3 (2028+): Review Birmingham entry post-SHROA clearance. Consider purpose-built 8–12 unit development

⚠️ Top Risks

  • Provider failure in lease-based model (very high)
  • Regulatory — 2027 licensing could shift the ground
  • HB policy — whole model depends on HB rates
  • Birmingham oversaturation — enforcement losing capacity
  • No funding for compliance costs
