📊 Key Learning Points

🔑 1. The Two-Stage Deal Funnel

"If you spend 5 hours on every property, you can't look at enough. You need a quick way to rule out the duds." — Ahmed Khan

The principle: Most investors spend too long analysing individual properties and not enough time filtering the market. The fix is a two-stage system:

  1. Stage 1 — 2-Minute Triage: Quick sense check. Price vs area average? Estimated refurb scope? Likely GDV? If the rough numbers don't pass, move on immediately. Target elimination, not analysis.
  2. Stage 2 — Detailed Analysis: Only proceed if Stage 1 passes. Full cost waterfall, 3+ comps, builder quotes, timeline planning, stress tests.

Application: When using PropAI Deals or Rightmove scans, apply a 3-factor triage (price-band fit × area demand × refurb signal) in under 2 minutes per property. Score ≥ 6/10 on triage → Stage 2 analysis.

🔑 2. Gross Yield ≠ Net Yield ≠ ROI — The Three Layers of Profitability

DealSheet AI's worked example of a Newcastle terraced house demonstrates exactly why conflating these metrics is dangerous:

MetricValueWhat It Tells You
Gross Yield6.33%"On paper, this looks decent." (ignores all costs)
Net Yield4.15%"The asset itself earns this after operating costs." (ignores leverage)
ROI (at 5% mortgage)2.1%"Your cash earns this." (the real return on your £56k investment)
ROI (at 3.5% mortgage)5.8%"Rate sensitivity dominates." (2.8× swing from 1.5% rate change)

Critical insight: A property that looks like a solid 6.33% gross yield can deliver just 2.1% return on capital when you account for leverage, costs, and tax. And a 1.5% change in mortgage rate more than DOUBLES the ROI.

Application: Every Deal Card must now show ALL three metrics side by side. Gross yield alone is a dangerous shortcut. Always calculate ROI using total cash invested, not just the headline price.

🔑 4. Bridging Finance — The BRRR Engine

Ahmed Khan uses bridging on every single BRRR project. Key insights:

  • Myth: "If the bridge expires, the lender repossesses immediately." Reality: Lenders prefer extensions and penalty fees — repossession is last resort.
  • But: Still aim to finish on time. Ahmed refinanced his first BRRR one day before the bridge expired.
  • Timeline is critical: Long refurb + bridging finance = high monthly interest. Sometimes paying a builder more to finish faster is cheaper overall.
  • Cash-only property purchases (short leases, structural issues) are accessible via bridging — this is where the best deals hide.

🧠 Best Deal Analysis Framework (Updated 2026-05-16)

Stage 0 — Triage (2 min per property, eliminates 80% of noise)

  1. Price check: Is this within your target range and below area average?
  2. Refurb signal: "Requires modernisation", "needs updating", or distress signals (probate, chain-free, 90+ days)? If "recently refurbished" → skip (no value-add).
  3. Strategy fit: Does the property type + area match a viable strategy? (BTL/BRRR/HMO/Flip)
  4. Quick yield: Estimate rent ÷ price. If gross yield < 5% → skip for WM BTL.
  5. Score triage 0-10: Pass if ≥ 6/10. Otherwise move on.

Stage 1 — Deal Identification

  1. Property must be under market value or distressed
  2. Check distress signals: probate, sitting tenant, price reductions, 90+ days on market
  3. Reject: recently refurbished (no value add), unmortgageable construction

Stage 2 — Market Comparison (Comparables)

  1. Minimum 3 comparables within ¼ mile, similar size/type/condition, sold within 12 months
  2. Cross-validate: Rightmove + Land Registry + Zoopla
  3. Apply evidence gap penalty for EPC below C, external condition issues
  4. EPC register for accurate square footage comparison

Stage 3 — Rental Analysis

  1. Search Rightmove lettings with 'let agreed' ticked — 35%+ ratio = strong demand
  2. Cross-reference: OpenRent for actual achieved rents
  3. Apply achievable rent, not aspirational
  4. Anchor institutions (hospitals, universities, transport) = built-in demand

Stage 4 — Full Cost Waterfall (Residual Method)

  1. Start from post-refurb GDV
  2. Deduct ALL costs: SDLT (+3% surcharge) + legal (£1.5k) + broker (£500) + survey (£500) + refurb + contingency (10%) + bridging finance (0.52-0.75%/mo) + holding costs + selling costs (2% + VAT)
  3. Deduct target profit margin (20% minimum)
  4. Result = Maximum Bid. If asking > max → REJECT.

Stage 5 — Calculate All Three Profit Layers

  1. Gross Yield = Annual Rent ÷ Purchase Price × 100
  2. Net Yield = Annual Net Income ÷ Total Investment Cost × 100
  3. Cash-on-Cash ROI = Annual Net Cashflow ÷ Total Cash Invested × 100
  4. Ceiling Price (BTL) = Price at which target net monthly cashflow is achieved

Stage 6 — Risk Analysis

  1. Stress test: +2% mortgage rate, -10% rent, +5% voids, +15% refurb overspend
  2. After-tax cashflow for personal-name BTL (Section 24 impact)
  3. Hidden cost buffer: professional management (£1,200-2,400/yr), maintenance escalation
  4. Mortgageability: standard construction? EPC ≥ C? Lease >80 years?
  5. Three exits: primary, secondary, tertiary — stress tested

Stage 7 — Verdict

  1. Score 0-10 using weighted factors (profitability 25%, cashflow 20%, risk 15%, financeability 10%, demand 10%, exit options 10%, compliance 10%)
  2. Only recommend action if score ≥ 8/10
  3. Deliver Deal Card with: all three yield metrics, Ceiling Price, max bid, top risk, hidden cost buffer

⚠️ Common Mistakes — Additions

🚩 Mistake: Confusing Gross Yield with Actual Returns

Problem: A 6.33% gross yield looks solid, but after full cost waterfall (SDLT, legal, refurb, mortgage, management, maintenance, voids, insurance), the actual cash-on-cash ROI can be as low as 2.1%. Investors rely on the headline number and never calculate the real return.

Fix: Every Deal Card must show gross yield, net yield, AND ROI side by side. If ROI is under 10%, the deal works for capital appreciation only — flag clearly.

🚩 Mistake: Spending Too Long on Bad Deals

Problem: Deep analysis on every property (5 hours each) means you can only assess a handful of deals. Most will be duds. The bottleneck isn't analysis depth — it's deal volume.

Fix: Implement a 2-minute triage (Stage 0). Only 20% of properties should pass to detailed analysis. Use a 3-factor quick score (price-fit × refurb-signal × area-demand) to gate which get Stage 2 attention.

🚩 Mistake: Not Factoring the Cost of DIY Management

Problem: Self-managing to "save" on agency fees costs $2,400-$6,000/year in hidden costs (extended vacancy, wrong pricing, slower tenant placement, deferred maintenance escalation, legal errors).

Fix: Every analysis should model two scenarios: self-managed vs professionally managed. The "saving" on management fees is often a net loss when hidden costs are accounted for.
