1. Class MA Planning Fee Indexation — April 2026

From 1 April 2026, all planning fees increased by 3.8% (CPI-linked indexation). Class MA prior approval fees rise from £250 to £260 per dwelling. The Planning Portal surcharge (£91.02 inc. VAT) can be bypassed by submitting directly to the LPA.

2. Sehgal v HMRC — Landmark Mixed-Use SDLT Ruling

Tribunal awarded £1.75M refund on £18.25M mixed-use purchase (apartment + parking + storage unit). The storage unit had its own title and was "legally and functionally separate." Confirms mixed-use SDLT planning remains viable post-MDR abolition. Key takeaway: Shop + flat above = strongest mixed-use claim. Separate title/lease essential.

3. Forge House, Brierley Hill — Class MA Controversy

A 1970s office block converted under Class MA (late 2023) became a crowded HMO with alleged fire safety concerns. Dudley Council refuses to answer questions. This is a reputational risk for the entire sector — if more poor-quality conversions emerge, WM councils may introduce Article 4 directions restricting Class MA.

4. Regent House, Dudley — 34-Bed HMO Recommended

Office-to-HMO conversion recommended for approval despite West Midlands Police objections citing 1,976 violent offences, 385 vehicle crimes, and 344 ASB incidents in the ward over 12 months. Developer's personal appeal addressed police concerns.

5. Class MA Housing Supply Data

Class MA conversions accelerating: 454 (22-23) → 803 (23-24) → 1,048 (24-25) = 130% growth. But total PDR change-of-use declining as office-to-resi market matures. Class MA underutilised in WM — less competition = better deals.

6. Class MA Design Challenges

Four critical hurdles: (a) Natural light — most common refusal reason, deep floorplates problematic; (b) Sound insulation — most expensive compliance item; (c) Thermal performance — budget £50-100/m² for upgrades; (d) External amenity — roof terrace or courtyard if feasible. Best shortcut: Target buildings originally built as residential, later converted to commercial.

7. Commercial Finance 2026

Three-stage path: Commercial mortgage (4-6%) → Bridging/development finance (0.75-1.5%/mo, 65-75% LTV) → BTL remortgage (4.5-5.5% IO). Promise Money offers development finance at 75% LTV with interest "above the line" (not deducted from advance) — gives more cash at day one.

1. High Street Crisis — The Opportunity Context

UK high streets are in structural decline, creating the buying opportunity: 13,000 shops closed in 2024 (Centre for Retail Research), 50,000+ businesses in critical financial distress (K2 Partners 2026), and high street vacancy remaining elevated in Q1 2026 (SHW). Most pronounced in the Midlands and North. Online spending now 25%+ of total retail (vs 3% in 2006). Cancer Research UK closing 88+ charity shops by May 2026 alone. Every shuttered shop is a potential Class MA conversion. Commercial values depressed in WM town centres — entry points at below-replacement cost.

2. Class MA Deep-Dive (2026 Update)

Since the September 2020 Use Class overhaul, Class E consolidates retail, offices, cafes, gyms, light industrial etc. into one super-class. The March 2024 changes removed two major barriers (1,500 sqm cap and 3-month vacancy), but the 2-year continuous Class E use rule remains the most common eligibility failure point. Councils strictly verify: they check business rates records, council tax records, and agent photos to prove the last 2 years.

3. Article 4 Risk — WM Councils Assessed

Research across all major WM councils confirms: No WM council currently has an Article 4 removing Class MA commercial-to-resi rights. WM Article 4s exclusively target HMO conversions (C3→C4). This is a FAVOURABLE SIGNAL for Class MA in the West Midlands — but with two caveats:

  • Councils KNOWN to restrict Class MA: Lambeth, Brent, Hillingdon, City of London (all London). This is a London-centric issue so far.
  • Risk of spread: As Class MA applications increase in the WM (driven by 2024 rule changes), councils may respond with Article 4s to protect employment floorspace. Check annually.
  • Key check for each target property: Is it in a conservation area? If so, ground-floor conversions face additional council scrutiny on 'character and appearance'.
  • Birmingham: City-wide HMO Article 4 since June 2020. Class MA for C3 still available. Conservation Area Article 4s for minor alterations exist in Edgbaston, Moseley, Harborne.
  • Dudley: HMO Article 4 (non-immediate, 2022). Class MA for C3 still available. No conservation area Article 4s affecting Class MA.
  • Wolverhampton, Sandwell, Walsall: No Article 4s affecting Class MA. Only HMO-specific restrictions.

5. SDLT Structuring — Mixed-Use Advantage (Deep-Dive)

Mixed-use properties (shop + flat above, or any building with both commercial and residential elements) are taxed at commercial SDLT rates. This is the single biggest tax advantage in commercial-to-resi.

PriceMixed-Use SDLTResidential BTL (+3%)Savings

£200,000£1,000£10,000£9,000
£250,000£3,500£12,500£9,000
£500,000£14,500£33,750£19,250

Key structuring rules:

  • Buy the whole property in one transaction — splitting into separate contracts may lose mixed-use treatment.
  • Document the non-residential element: floorplans, rateable value, photos. HMRC may query if the commercial portion is very small.
  • Jan 2026 Tax Tribunal: Clyde & Co reports a case where combined purchase of resi apartment + parking space + storage unit was successfully argued as mixed-use, saving £1.75M in SDLT. This confirms the principle is alive but HMRC is scrutinising.
  • Risk: Government consultation (Nov 2021) proposed apportionment-based SDLT for mixed-use — residential portion charged at resi rates. Not implemented yet but could be resurrected. Act while the current regime lasts.

7. The 9 Prior Approval Matters — How to Succeed

  1. Transport: Most high streets already served — low risk. Prepare a simple transport statement.
  2. Contamination: Phase 1 desk study (£800–£2,000) for any former retail/industrial building.
  3. Flooding: Check Flood Zone 2/3 via gov.uk flood map. If in zone, commission FRA (£500–£1,500).
  4. Noise: If above continuing commercial, commission acoustic assessment (£500–£1,200). Mitigate with secondary glazing.
  5. Natural light: MOST COMMON REFUSAL. Deep-plan buildings (>8m) struggle. Commission BRE daylight assessment early (£1k–£3k). Restrict unit depth.
  6. Fire safety: Only if >18m or creates 2+ dwellings. Budget £2k–£5k for fire engineering.
  7. Conservation area: Only ground floor changes in CA. Prepare character assessment.
  8. Industrial conflict: Only if adjacent to heavy industry — rare on high streets.
  9. Nursery/health centre: Only if last use was nursery or health centre — impact assessment needed.

1. Class MA Framework (2026)

Class MA (Town and Country Planning GPDO 2015 as amended) allows change of use from Use Class E (commercial, business, service) to Class C3 (residential dwellings) without full planning permission. Replaced narrower Class O (offices only) in 2021.

Key Changes (5 March 2024)

  • REMOVED Vacancy requirement — occupied buildings can now be converted
  • REMOVED 1,500 m² floor area limit — any size now convertible
  • RETAINED 2-year continuous Class E use rule
  • RETAINED Prior approval still mandatory — 56-day decision window
  • EXCLUDED Listed buildings, scheduled monuments, SSSI, safety zones

Prior Approval Matters Assessed

  1. Transport & highways
  2. Contamination risks
  3. Flooding (Flood Zone 2/3 → FRA required)
  4. Noise from adjacent commercial uses
  5. Natural light to all habitable rooms
  6. Fire safety (>18m buildings need fire report)
  7. Sustainability of area (loss of commercial floorspace)
  8. Housing design & space standards

Cost: £120–£125/dwelling application fee. Deemed consent if no decision within 56 days.

2. Article 4 Direction Risks

  • Lambeth (London): 3 non-immediate Article 4 directions withdrawing Class MA in defined areas
  • Kingston (London): Proposed for primary shopping areas
  • Multiple London boroughs: Various — mainly targeting town centre retail cores
  • Chorley: Immediate Article 4 blocking HMOs
  • Dudley: Borough-wide HMO Article 4 (effective Sept 2023) — only blocks C3-to-HMO, NOT Class MA C3 conversion. Class MA still available in Dudley for C3 residential. However, any C3 created via MA cannot later convert to HMO without full planning.
  • Rule of thumb: Most A4 targets primary retail frontages. Secondary high streets and upper floors are safer.
