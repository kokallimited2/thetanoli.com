📊 Market Overview

16 days since RRA went live. 15 days until the Information Sheet deadline.

The biggest building story this week is the 31 May 2026 RRA Information Sheet deadline — every landlord in England must provide the government's official Information Sheet to existing tenants or face fines up to £7,000. With only 15 days left, thousands of non-professional landlords are scrambling. This creates a second compliance-driven wave of landlord exits and management outsourcing — directly feeding supply into the management partnership pipeline.

New this session: 131,140 households in temporary accommodation (+12% YoY, £2.8Bn); councils seeking multi-year procurement frameworks with professional operators; Social & Affordable Homes Programme £27.3Bn confirmed; Awaab's Law Phase 2 coming October 2026; 1M+ empty homes across England; RRA info sheet deadline panic driving landlord outsourcing decisions.

🔥 6 New Findings

  1. RRA Info Sheet Deadline — 31 May 2026 (15 days away): All existing tenants must receive the official government Information Sheet. Failure = up to £7,000 fine. The Independent Landlord reports this is a legal requirement with no exceptions for verbal tenancies. Impact: Thousands of disorganised/non-compliant landlords will panic — some will sell, some will hand management to professionals, some will exit PRS entirely. Direct feed of stock into guaranteed rent / management partnership pipeline.
  2. 132k Households in Temporary Accommodation — £2.8Bn Spent: Near-record high. 25% cost rise on previous year. London accounts for >50%. Guardian reports councils' costs will double to £4Bn by 2029-30. Impact: Councils are structurally dependent on private rented sector procurement. This is a multi-decade demand driver for guaranteed rent, supported housing, and B2B management partnership models.
  3. £27.3Bn Social & Affordable Homes Programme 2026-2036: Government's long-term housing strategy confirmed. Awaab's Law Phase 2 extends to damp, mould, excess cold, falls, fire in October 2026. Impact: Councils shifting from reactive emergency placements to preferred supplier frameworks with multi-year contracts. Professional operators with compliance systems have structural advantage over individual landlords.
  4. 1M+ Empty Homes in England: Action on Empty Homes analysis of council tax data: 754,264 empty homes (300,000+ long-term), 268,153 second homes. Impact: Direct targeting opportunity for management partnerships — tired landlords of empty properties are prime candidates. The 300,000+ long-term empties represent a massive untapped pool for guaranteed rent / corporate let conversion.
  5. Coventry Emerging as HS2 Support Corridor: Maine Stays and multiple operators positioning Coventry as "smart base" for HS2 crews — lower property prices than Solihull/Birmingham, A45/A46/M6 access to Curzon Street, Interchange, Water Orton. Impact: CV postcodes represent a lower-cost entry point than B92 with the same HS2 contractor demand profile.
  6. Social Housing Procurement Shifting to 5-10 Year Agreements: Link Property and Apex confirm councils increasingly offering multi-year volume commitments, preferred supplier frameworks, digitised compliance reporting. Impact: Operators who invest in compliance infrastructure (fire safety, HHSRS, EPC, licensing) gain exclusive access to government-backed demand pipelines that individual landlords can't compete with.
