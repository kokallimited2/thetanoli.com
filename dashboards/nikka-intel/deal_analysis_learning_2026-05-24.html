📐 The RICS ESG 4th Edition × Valuation Gap — A NEW Compounding Effect

The RICS ESG 4th Edition (April 2026) mandates surveyors to quantify ESG gaps explicitly. Properties below EPC C face a direct capital value discount. When combined with the standard valuation gap, this creates a DUAL-FACTOR penalty:

Combined Refi Value = ARV × Standard Gap Factor (0.85-0.95) × ESG Factor (varies)
ESG Factors:
EPC A/B → 1.00 (no penalty)
EPC C → 0.95 (5% ESG discount)
EPC D → 0.90 (10% ESG discount)
EPC E or below → 0.85 (15% ESG discount ~ £10k retrofit cost)
Example: £180k ARV, EPC D, standard gap factor 0.90
Combined: £180k × 0.90 × 0.90 = £145,800 vs £180k (full ARV)
At 75% LTV: £109,350 vs £135,000 → £25,650 less refinance capital

Impact: A property with EPC D or below in 2026 faces approximately 15-20% combined valuation haircut (standard gap 5-15% + ESG penalty 5-15%). This was not visible before RICS ESG 4th Edition. Every BRRR analysis must now check EPC band before finalising ARV assumptions.

🔴 Void Costs: The Hidden Quarterly Tax

  • Single void = 8%+ of annual return wiped out in 30 days
  • Unlike rent reductions, voids are pure dead weight — no cash flow, no capital gain, no mortgage cover
  • Renters' Rights Act 2026 shift to periodic tenancies adds exit uncertainty — tenants now have more freedom to leave
  • New rule: Model voids at 1 month/year minimum (8.33%). For high-turnover areas (student, young professional), model at 1.5-2 months/year (12.5-16.7%)

Updated flow: The Valuation Pack strategy changes from "nice to have" to "non-negotiable" — without it, the surveyor defaults to conservative AVM and you lose 5-15% immediately.

Step 0 — Pre-Acquisition Checks

  1. Check EPC band — If below C, apply ESG penalty to ARV. Budget £10k upgrade if D, £15k+ if E or below.
  2. Prepare Valuation Pack before even viewing the property:
    • Schedule of Works (itemised, with costs)
    • Before/After photos of similar completed projects
    • 3 recent sold comparables (within ¼ mile, similar type/size)
    • Building control completion certs from previous works
    • Structural warranties (10-year preferred)
    • EPC certificate (post-refurb projection if improvement planned)
  3. Apply triple-factor refinance model — Do not assume you'll get full ARV × 75%. Model at ARV × GapFactor × ESGFactor × LTV.
  4. Determine if the deal survives without full capital recycling — Can you tolerate leaving £15k-£25k in the deal AND still achieve target ROCE?

New: Dual-Factor Valuation for BRRR

Adjusted Refi Value = ARV × StandardGapFactor × ESGFactor
StandardGapFactor = 0.85 (high risk) / 0.90 (standard) / 0.95 (low risk)

Refi Proceeds = Adjusted Refi Value × 0.75 (max LTV)
Money Left In = Total All-In Cost − Refi Proceeds

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Building your ARV on open market comps, not mortgage valuation comps — These are fundamentally different. The surveyor values for lending risk, not market potential. Your "identical" £185k comps become £165k at refinance. (Sources: Property Filter, Sondr, TMS — all confirm this)
  • Ignoring the void cost multiplier — A 1-month void isn't just 8.3% income loss. It's 8.3% income + £400+ in added costs + lost mortgage contributions + tenant-finding time. Total damage = £1,200-1,500 on typical WM rent.
  • Assuming gross yield tells you anything about viability — Residence Index UK (Apr 2026): "Gross yield is not a return. It is the starting point of a calculation that is frequently left unfinished." Net yields fall 1.5-3% below gross. A 6.5% gross can deliver 4-5% net.
  • Not preparing a Valuation Pack before the surveyor visit — TMS: "You do not renovate for the tenant. You renovate for the surveyor." Without a pack, the surveyor defaults to conservative AVM. A strong pack "completely removes the surveyor's ability to default to conservative valuation."
  • Over-specifying refurb above local market ceiling — Italian marble in a mid-market area won't increase the valuation. Surveyors only give credit for what the local market supports. Renovate to the street standard, not your personal preference.
  • Modelling voids as a theoretical cost, not a structural one — Residence Index UK: "Void periods are not occasional inconvenience. They are a structural feature of the asset class." The shift to periodic tenancies (Renters' Rights Act) makes voids more likely, not less.

⚡ Today's Improvement

Improvement #10 — Mandatory Dual-Factor Valuation Gap for All BRRR Refinance Calculations

What: Replace the single Valuation Gap Factor (0.85-0.95) with a DUAL-FACTOR model that also accounts for the RICS ESG 4th Edition mandate (April 2026). The ESG factor specifically penalises EPC below C.

Why: The standard valuation gap (surveyor vs open market) is market consensus across three 2026 sources. The RICS ESG mandate adds a NEW, systematic downward pressure that was invisible before April 2026. Combined, properties with EPC D or below face a 15-20% combined valuation haircut — enough to turn a "recycle 80% of capital" deal into a capital trap.

How to apply: Before every BRRR refinance calculation: (1) Check EPC band. (2) Apply Standard Gap Factor (0.85-0.95). (3) Apply ESG Factor (1.00 for A/B, 0.95 for C, 0.90 for D, 0.85 for E+). (4) Model Money Left In at the combined factor. (5) If Money Left In exceeds £20k at combined factor → the deal is a capital trap.

Convergence validation: Property Filter (Apr 2026) + Sondr (Feb 2026) + TMS UK Properties (2026) + RICS (Apr 2026) — four sources converge on the same structural problem: valuation gaps compound and are no longer avoidable.

🎯 Application

How to use this immediately in deal analysis:

  1. EPC check first: Before any financial modelling, look up the property's EPC. If below C, the ARV needs a 5-15% ESG penalty baked in from Day 1.
  2. Valuation Pack template: Create a standardised VAP (Valuation Argument Pack) template that can be filled out for every BRRR candidate. Include: Schedule of Works, 3 Sold Comps, Before Concepts/After Projections, Building Control Pathway, EPC Upgrade Plan.
  3. Void uplift for WM: Change default void assumption from 1 month/yr (8.33%) to 1.5 months/yr (12.5%) for all West Midlands BTL/BRRR analyses. West Midlands void periods are structurally longer than the UK average.
  4. Reject deals that only survive at full ARV × 75% LTV: If the deal produces positive Money Left In (£0 or less) only at the most optimistic ARV × 0.95 × 1.00 × 0.75, reject it. The realistic scenario is ARV × 0.90 × 0.95 × 0.75.
