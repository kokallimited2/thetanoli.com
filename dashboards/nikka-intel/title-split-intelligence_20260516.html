📜 COMMONHOLD BILL DEEP ANALYSIS — Jones Day Confirms Key Details

Jones Day (May 2026): Comprehensive analysis of the Draft Commonhold & Leasehold Reform Bill (published 27 Jan 2026):

  • Ban on new long leases >21 years for flats — all new flats must be commonhold. Exceptions: purpose-built rental blocks (student/BTR).
  • £250/year ground rent cap for 40 years — then peppercorn. Existing leaseholders protected from aggressive ground rent escalation.
  • Forfeiture abolished — landlords can no longer terminate a long lease as remedy for breach. Must pursue "lease enforcement claim" in court.
  • 50% conversion threshold — existing leaseholders can convert to commonhold with simple majority (down from 100% unanimity, which made 2002 Act unworkable).
  • Violation of ban = lessee converts at no cost, lessor faces financial penalties

Implication: Window is closing for traditional leasehold splits. If you act in 2026, 999-year peppercorn leases remain valid. Once Bill becomes law (est. 2027), new flats must use commonhold structure. Existing leases are unaffected indefinitely.

📜 Commonhold & Leasehold Reform Bill — Q&A for Title Split Investors

QuestionAnswer
Will the Bill ban leasehold title splitting?Eventually, yes. New long leases (>21 years) for flats will be banned. Newly-created flats must use commonhold structure instead. Existing leases unaffected.
Can I still do freehold→leasehold splits in 2026?YES, but act now. Bill is in pre-legislative scrutiny. Transitional period not yet settled. Traditional leasehold model works for splits commenced in 2026.
What if I already have a 999-year lease?You're fine. All existing leases remain valid indefinitely. The Bill expressly preserves existing leasehold interests.
What would a commonhold title split look like?Instead of granting leases, you'd grant commonhold units. Each unit owner owns the freehold of their unit outright + membership in a commonhold association that owns common areas. Similar to US condominium.
Ground rent impact?£250/year cap for 40 years on existing leases, then peppercorn. New leases already restricted to peppercorn under 2022 Act.
Forfeiture gone?YES — long lease forfeiture abolished. Landlords need court "lease enforcement claim" instead. Major change to landlord-tenant power balance.
