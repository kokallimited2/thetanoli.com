📜 Renters' Rights Act Now in Force — Bidding Bans Reshape Market

STRENGTH 8/10
🔗 Gov.uk · NRLA · Davies & Partners · Housing Hand · 1 May 2026

🔍 Why It Matters

The RRA came into effect 1 May 2026 — the biggest rental reform in decades. Section 21 ("no-fault") evictions abolished. Periodic tenancies become the default. Landlords must serve official Information Sheet to all existing tenants by 31 May. Offering above asking rent is now banned — previously ~1 in 10 tenancies went above asking. Landlords are responding by setting higher upfront asking rents, which will mechanically lift averages. The student HMO sector gets a specific carve-out: landlords can use Ground 4A to terminate for student intake rotation (2 months' notice).

💡 Actionable Opportunity

  • HMO strategy strengthened: RRA makes tenant management harder for single-lets (can't evict easily), but HMO with professional management is less exposed. Student HMO specifically gets Ground 4A protection.
  • Rent-setting shift: With bidding banned, landlords will anchor rents higher. Q3-Q4 2026 asking rents likely to jump 3-5% as the new equilibrium forms. Factor this into ERV projections.
  • Supported housing unaffected: RRA exempts exempt accommodation (SEA) and tenancies with care/support — another tailwind for the supported housing thesis.
  • Risk for standard BTL: One problem tenant now costs far more to remove. Factor 2-3 months' void + legal costs into BTL cashflow.
