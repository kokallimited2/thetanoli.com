⚠️ New Mistakes Archive (2026-05-22)

Mistake #1 — "Blind Confidence" Meta-Mistake (PIN/Simon Zutshi)

Not knowing what you don't know. Every mistake in property investing traces back to acting on assumptions, incomplete knowledge, or advice from professionals who lack specific property investment experience. Fix: Always ask "What am I missing?" before committing capital. Explicitly label a deal as "Blind Confidence" if all inputs are optimistic.

Mistake #2 — Skipping the £700 Survey That Could Save £20k (DBR Invest)

RICS-accredited survey is non-negotiable. A £700 survey can reveal hidden damp, structural cracks, or roof issues that cost £20k+ to fix — and provides leverage for price negotiation or walking away from a bad deal.

Mistake #3 — Chasing Past Performance Hotspots (10 Acre)

"Everyone rushes to yesterday's winners." Manchester was oversupplied by 2025 — investors who bought there in 2024-25 are competing for tenants. The winning 2026-28 play is Birmingham HS2 corridor and Liverpool docks, where supply is still constrained and demand is growing.

Mistake #4 — Ignoring the 2027 Tax Regime Shift (10 Acre)

From 2027, property income tax jumps to 22-47% (currently 19-25% Corp Tax for Ltd Co, 20-45% personal with Section 24 drag). The gap widens significantly. Any deal modelled on current tax rates without accounting for 2027 changes is fragile. Fix: Model post-2027 tax liability now, particularly for Ltd Co structures.

Mistake #5 — Assuming LTV Will Hold at Modelled Levels (PropStream + Property Filter)

Three separate findings: (1) Most lenders cap BRRR refinance at 80% LTV (2026 norm). (2) At 80-85% LTV, rental coverage fails at stress test rates (5.5-7%) even when 75% LTV passes. (3) 6-12 month seasoning of rental income may be required before refinance approval. Fix: Model refinance at 75% LTV (not 80%) AND model with a 6-month rental seasoning period that extends bridging.

⚡ Today's Improvement — Add "Blind Spot Audit" Gate (Stage 0c)

🧠 New Gate: Blind Spot Audit (Stage 0c — Post-0b, Pre-0 Triage)

Source: Simon Zutshi / PIN ("Blind confidence is more dangerous than uncertainty") + Property Filter (quantified gap between optimistic and reality: 62.5% capital trap magnification)

Why it's needed: Every framework step so far focuses on what to calculate. None systematically challenges whether your assumptions are valid in the first place. The #1 cause of bad deals is not bad maths — it's optimistic inputs entered into otherwise correct formulas. A 62.5% capital trap doesn't come from one error; it comes from multiple optimistic assumptions compounding.

The Blind Spot Audit is a structured pre-modelling gate with 4 checks:

  1. Input Uncertainty Quantification — For EVERY numeric input (rent, refurb cost, void rate, ARV, timeline, mortgage rate), state THREE values: optimistic / base / pessimistic. If you can't estimate a range for any input, label that input as SPECULATIVE — it has insufficient evidence to support any value.
  2. Source Quality Audit — For each input, grade the data source:
    • A-grade: Written quotation or official data (builder quote, Land Registry sold price, lender offer letter)
    • B-grade: Well-researched estimate (3+ comparable rents from let-agreed data, verbal contractor estimate)
    • C-grade: Guess or single data point (one Rightmove listing, "my mate said," generic area average)
    If any key input (refurb cost, ARV, rent) is C-grade → the analysis is speculative, not analytical.
  3. "What Would Change My Mind" Statement — Write one explicit sentence: "I would reject this deal if [specific condition] happens." Examples:
    • "I would reject this BRRR if the surveyor values below £170k."
    • "I would reject this BTL if rent drops below £725/mo."
    • "I would reject this flip if the refurb timeline exceeds 5 months."
    This forces pre-defined exit criteria rather than post-hoc rationalisation.
  4. Blind Confidence Flag — If every input in the base case is at the optimistic end of the range (highest rent, zero voids, cheapest refurb, rate cuts assumed), label the deal with 🟡 BLIND CONFIDENCE — Full stress testing required before proceeding. This is a formalised version of "if it looks too good to be true, it probably is."

Integration into existing framework: The Blind Spot Audit runs AFTER Stage 0b (Two-Loan Alignment) and BEFORE Stage 0 (4-Factor Triage). It's independent of deal type — every property analysis, whether BTL, BRRR, Flip, HMO, or Commercial, must pass this audit.

Expected impact: By forcing explicit uncertainty quantification before any model inputs are used, the Blind Spot Audit prevents the #1 investor mistake — optimistic bias. This alone should eliminate ~30-50% of false-positive deal scores (deals that look good on paper but fail in reality).
