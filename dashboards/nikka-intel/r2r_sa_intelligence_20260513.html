📋 Daily R2R & Serviced Accommodation Intelligence — 2026-05-13

📊 Market Overview

Date: 13 May 2026 — The Renters' Rights Act took effect 12 days ago (May 1st). The market is still rippling from its implementation, and the R2R/SA landscape has shifted decisively.

The big picture: 2026 is the year the R2R and SA sectors split into two distinct tracks. The "easy money" era (2016–2024) where anyone could lease a property, list on Airbnb, and pocket the spread is definitively over. Multiple structural forces have converged:

  • Renters' Rights Act (May 1, 2026): Section 21 abolished, periodic tenancies mandatory, two-month notice minimum, rent bidding banned — the legal scaffolding that made R2R HMO work has been demolished
  • FHL tax regime abolished (April 2025): SA operators can no longer deduct 100% of mortgage interest — now hit by Section 24, same as BTL. Cash-rich, low-leverage operators survive; leveraged operators are underwater
  • Platform fee escalation: Airbnb forced 15.5% host fee on professional hosts. Booking.com charges 15–25% commission. Margin compression across the board
  • Leisure SA saturation: Feb 2026 occupancy hit 37%. August 2025 bookings fell 5% YoY. Supply grew 2.3% while demand contracted

But: The B2B mid-term corporate let market is booming. National corporate housing market is £2.8 billion — up 34% year-on-year. HS2 infrastructure spending is driving massive contractor demand. The "missing middle" (2-week to 3-month stays) is the single biggest unfilled gap in UK accommodation right now.

⚠️ WARNING: Standard R2R Is Dead in 2026

This is consensus across all 10+ sources researched today. Here's why:

  • Profit collapse: Average profit per month is now £300–£500 before unexpected costs. One boiler breakdown, one compliance fine, or one eviction wipes out an entire year's profit
  • No Section 21: If a tenant stops paying, you're in a 6–12 month legal battle. During which you still pay the landlord their fixed rent
  • Zero wealth building: R2R produces no capital growth, no equity — temporary cashflow with 100% liability
  • Inexperienced operators flooding the market: Low barriers to entry meant thousands of undercapitalised, under-qualified operators are now failing — tarnishing the model for everyone
  • Foot Forward Properties (Feb 2026): "The strategy is shrinking because regulation is tightening, enforcement is becoming sharper, and the sector has become crowded with inexperienced operators"
  • Simon Zutshi (PIN, 2026): "Rent to rent will come to an end in 2026. The numbers no longer stack, the risk has overtaken the reward"
