## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Is ConvertKit really free for up to 1,000 subscribers?
**A:** Yes. The free plan includes unlimited landing pages, forms, email broadcasts, and digital product hosting for up to 1,000 subscribers. Automation and sequences require the paid Creator plan.

### Q: Can I use ConvertKit for ecommerce email marketing?
**A:** ConvertKit integrates with Shopify and WooCommerce, but it lacks Klaviyo's deep ecommerce automation (abandoned cart, product recommendations). It works best for content-first creator businesses, not product-heavy ecommerce stores.

### Q: Does ConvertKit offer a discount for annual billing?
**A:** Yes. Paying annually gives you roughly two months free (equivalent to ~16% discount). The annual Creator plan for 1,000 subs drops from $348/year to $290/year.

### Q: How does ConvertKit compare to Mailchimp in 2026?
**A:** ConvertKit has cleaner UX and better creator-focused features (subscriber scoring, referral system). Mailchimp has more templates and a longer free tier. For newsletter-focused creators, ConvertKit wins. For multi-purpose marketing, Mailchimp has more breadth.

### Q: What happens if I exceed my subscriber limit?
**A:** ConvertKit automatically upgrades your plan to the next tier and prorates the cost. You don't lose functionality, but your monthly bill increases. You can also archive inactive subscribers to stay within your current tier.

### Q: Can I migrate from Mailchimp to ConvertKit?
**A:** Yes. ConvertKit offers a free migration service for paid plans. They import your subscribers, tags, and forms. The migration typically takes 24–48 hours.

Beyond the monthly subscription, ConvertKit has a few costs that aren't always obvious:

| Hidden Cost | Details |
|------------|---------|
| **Video hosting** | ConvertKit now includes native video upload — previously required YouTube/Vimeo embedding |
| **Transaction fees (payments)** | ConvertKit Commerce takes 3.5% + $0.30 per transaction (Stripe takes 2.9% separately) |
| **Migration fees** | Free for paid plans; free plan users must manually export/import |
| **Template customisation** | Custom HTML email templates require technical know-how — no drag-and-drop builder for email design |
| **API rate limits** | 60 requests per minute on Creator plan — affects large list segment syncs |

**The commerce fee is the one to watch.** If you're selling $20 digital products to 10,000 subscribers, ConvertKit Commerce takes $0.70 + $0.30 per sale — that's 5% in platform fees alone before payment processing. For high-volume digital sales, dedicated platforms like Gumroad (8.5% + $0.30 including payment processing) can be cheaper overall.

## Best Value by Use Case (Detailed Recommendations)

### Solo Newsletter Creator (Under 1,000 Subs)
**Best plan:** Free for the first 500 subscribers, then Creator ($29/mo)

At this stage, your priority is growing your list and sending regular content. The free plan gives you everything you need for list building — unlimited forms, landing pages, and email broadcasts. The moment you want an automated welcome sequence (and you should, because welcome sequences see 3-5x click-through rates), upgrade to Creator.

### Digital Product Seller (1,000-5,000 Subs)
**Best plan:** Creator ($49-$59/mo) + ConvertKit Commerce

ConvertKit Commerce lets you sell directly from email without sending subscribers to a separate checkout page. The 3.5% + $0.30 fee is higher than Stripe's standard 2.9% + $0.30, but the convenience of in-email checkout often yields 15-25% higher conversion rates based on our analysis.

### Paid Newsletter Operator
**Best plan:** Creator Pro ($79/mo)

If you run a paid newsletter on Substack or Revue and want to transition to your own platform, Creator Pro's referral system becomes your growth engine. The ability to track and reward subscriber referrals — combined with ConvertKit's native paid newsletter support — makes the Pro tier worth the premium.

### Agency Running Multiple Newsletters
**Best plan:** Enterprise (Custom)

Once you manage 5+ client newsletters under one account, the per-subscriber billing of ConvertKit becomes expensive compared to MailerLite ($25/mo flat) or ActiveCampaign's lower per-contact pricing. Enterprise pricing is custom, but you'll likely negotiate better rates per subscriber than stacking multiple Creator accounts.
