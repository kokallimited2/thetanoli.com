## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which is more important: VPN, antivirus, or password manager?
**All three serve different purposes.** If you can only start with one, get a **password manager** first — weak credentials are the most commonly exploited vulnerability. Then add a **VPN** for privacy, and finally an **antivirus** for malware protection. But the complete stack is much stronger than any single tool.

### Can I use a free VPN instead of a paid one?
Free VPNs have serious drawbacks: data caps, slow speeds, and in many cases, they **sell your data** to make money — the exact opposite of what a VPN should do. Stick with trusted paid options like NordVPN or ExpressVPN.

### Do I need both antivirus and a VPN?
Yes. They protect against different threats. Your antivirus stops malware from running on your device. Your VPN encrypts your internet traffic and prevents network-level snooping. A VPN won't stop a virus, and antivirus won't encrypt your traffic.

### Is Bitwarden safe since it's free?
Yes. Bitwarden is open-source, independently audited (cure53, 2023), and uses zero-knowledge encryption. It's one of the most transparent and well-reviewed password managers available.

### How often should I change my passwords?
In 2026, the better approach is to use **unique, complex passwords for every account** (generated by your password manager) rather than regularly changing the same weak passwords. If a service you use is breached, change that specific password immediately — your password manager's breach monitoring should alert you.

### What should I do if my data was exposed in the Canvas breach?
Change your Canvas password immediately. Enable two-factor authentication. Use a password manager to generate a unique password. Consider freezing your credit and using identity theft protection. For students, this is particularly important since exposed data often includes SSNs, addresses, and academic records.
