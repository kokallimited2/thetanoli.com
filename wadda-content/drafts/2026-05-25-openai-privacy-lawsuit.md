> **Last updated: May 25, 2026, 06:00 UTC**

OpenAI is facing a **class-action privacy lawsuit** alleging that the company shared ChatGPT user data with Google and Meta without consent. The lawsuit claims that over **200 million ChatGPT users** may have had their conversations, prompts, and personal information transmitted to third-party advertising platforms.

This isn't just another data breach story. This is about **how your AI conversations are being used** — and who's profiting from them.

## What Data Did OpenAI Share?

According to the lawsuit, the data shared with Google and Meta includes:

- **Conversation transcripts** — Your actual prompts and ChatGPT's responses
- **User account information** — Email addresses, IP addresses, device identifiers
- **Usage patterns** — How often you use ChatGPT, what features you access
- **Search behavior** — Topics and themes you explore
- **Technical data** — Browser type, operating system, session duration

### What Was NOT Shared
- Payment information (OpenAI handles this through Stripe directly)
- Training data used in model development

## How Your ChatGPT Conversations May Have Been Used

The data was shared via **tracking pixels** and **API integrations** embedded in ChatGPT's web interface and mobile app. These are the same tracking technologies used across millions of websites to serve targeted advertising.

The alleged flow:

1. You type a prompt into ChatGPT
2. OpenAI's interface loads tracking scripts from Google and Meta
3. These scripts capture: your IP, browser fingerprint, session activity, and — critically — **the context of your conversation**
4. This data is sent to Google and Meta's advertising platforms
5. You start seeing ads related to your ChatGPT conversations

**Example:** If you asked ChatGPT about "vacation planning" or "business software for a new startup," you might see ads for travel agencies or CRM tools shortly after.

## The Legal Case: What the Lawsuit Alleges

### Key Allegations

| Claim | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Wiretap Act violations** | Intercepting user communications without consent |
| **Privacy Act violations** | Sharing personally identifiable information with third parties |
| **Deceptive practices** | Privacy policy did not disclose data sharing with advertisers |
| **Unjust enrichment** | OpenAI profited from user data shared without compensation |

### Potential Damages

- Statutory damages: $100-$1,000 per violation per user
- With 200 million users involved: **potentially trillions of dollars**
- Reduced to realistic estimates: **$500 million - $2 billion** settlement range

### Jurisdiction

The lawsuit was filed in the **Northern District of California** — home to some of the most privacy-friendly court rulings in US history.

## OpenAI's Response

OpenAI has issued a statement denying the allegations:

> "OpenAI takes user privacy seriously. We do not share personal data with Google or Meta for advertising purposes. Our privacy policy clearly describes how we use data to improve our services."

However, the lawsuit has already triggered:
- Investigations by data protection authorities in Europe (GDPR implications)
- Preliminary inquiries from the FTC
- Calls for Congressional hearings on AI data practices

The company has also announced several privacy-focused changes in recent months — though critics argue these are reactive rather than proactive.

3. **Disable "Improve the model for everyone"** (this stops using your conversations for training)
4. **Delete your chat history** (click the trash icon)
5. **Export your data** (Settings → Data Controls → Export Data)

**Q: How do I know if my ChatGPT data was shared?**
A: You can't directly verify this. The safest approach is to assume your data was shared and take protective measures.

**Q: Should I stop using ChatGPT?**
A: Not necessarily. Use it with privacy protections: VPN enabled, privacy settings configured, and separate AI-specific accounts.

**Q: I want to delete all my ChatGPT data — how?**

**Q: What about other AI tools — are they doing the same thing?**
