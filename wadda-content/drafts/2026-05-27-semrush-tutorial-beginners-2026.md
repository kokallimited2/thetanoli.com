## Solution: Step-by-Step Tutorials

### 1. Keyword Research — Find the Right Keywords

**Why it matters**: Targeting the wrong keywords wastes months of effort. Semrush's keyword research tools remove the guesswork.

**Step-by-step:**

1. Go to **Keyword Overview**
2. Enter a seed keyword (e.g., "VPN for privacy")
3. Review the results:
   - **Volume** — Monthly searches (500-5,000 is the sweet spot for new sites)
   - **KD%** — Keyword Difficulty (under 30% = achievable for new sites)
   - **CPC** — Cost-per-click (high CPC = commercial intent = conversion potential)
   - **Intent** — Informational, Commercial, Transactional, Navigational
4. Click **Keyword Magic Tool** → generates 1,000+ related keywords in seconds
5. Filter by KD% (under 30%), volume (200+), and intent (Commercial for money pages)
6. Export your list → organize by content type: blog posts (informational), landing pages (commercial), product pages (transactional)

**Pro tip**: Use the **Keyword Gap** tool to find keywords your competitors rank for but you don't. These are the fastest wins.

### 2. Competitor Analysis — Spy on Your Competition

**Why it matters**: Your competitors already did the research. Learn from their successes and exploit their gaps.

**Step-by-step:**

1. Go to **Domain Analytics → Domain Overview**
2. Enter your domain → click **Compare** → add 3-5 competitors
3. Review:
   - **Traffic distribution** — Which pages get the most organic traffic?
   - **Top keywords** — Which keywords drive their traffic?
   - **Keyword Gap** — Keywords they rank for that you don't
4. Click **Organic Research → Positions** → sort by volume to see their best pages
5. Click each page → analyze the content structure, headings, and backlinks

**Pro tip**: Sort competitors by traffic trend (upward) — these are the ones doing something right. Study their recent content.

### 3. Site Audit — Fix Technical SEO Issues

**Why it matters**: Even perfect content won't rank if your site has technical SEO problems. Semrush's Site Audit catches everything.

**Step-by-step:**

1. Go to **Project → Site Audit**
2. Run audit (5-10 minutes for most sites)
3. Review the **Crawlability** report: blocked pages, redirect chains, broken links
4. Check **HTTPS** issues: mixed content, expired certificates
5. Review **Core Web Vitals**: LCP (<2.5s), FID (<100ms), CLS (<0.1)
6. Check **Mobile usability**: responsive design, touch targets, font sizes
7. Export **Issues report** → prioritize by Error > Warning > Notice

**Health targets**: 90%+ Site Health score, 0 Crawlability errors, All Core Web Vitals passing.

**Pro tip**: Set up weekly automated audits → email alerts when score drops below 85%.

### 4. Content Optimization — Write Content That Ranks

**Why it matters**: Semrush's SEO Content Template gives you a data-backed content brief based on your target keyword.

**Step-by-step:**

1. Go to **SEO Content Template**
2. Enter your target keyword
3. Review the recommendations:
   - **Related terms** — Semantic keywords to include
   - **Readability score** — Target score based on top-ranking competitors
   - **Links count** — How many internal/external links top pages use
   - **Content length** — Word count of top 10 results
4. Write your content following the template
5. Use **SEO Writing Assistant** (browser extension) to score your draft in real-time:
   - Target: 80+ SEO score, 70+ Readability, 60+ Originality

### 5. Link Building — Find Backlink Opportunities

**Why it matters**: Backlinks are still the #1 ranking factor. Semrush helps you find opportunities your competitors are using.

**Step-by-step:**

1. Go to **Backlink Analytics**
2. Enter a competitor's domain
3. Review:
   - **Referring domains** — Total unique domains linking to them
   - **Authority Score** — Average link quality (50+ is strong)
   - **New/Lost backlinks** — Recent changes in their profile
4. Click **Backlink Gap** → find domains linking to multiple competitors but not to you
5. Export the list → build outreach campaigns targeting those domains

**Pro tip**: Focus on "Lost backlinks" from competitors — those domains already showed willingness to link to similar content.
