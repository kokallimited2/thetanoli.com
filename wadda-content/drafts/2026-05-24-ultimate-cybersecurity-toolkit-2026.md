## Category 1: VPN Services — Best VPNs for Privacy & Security

### Why You Need a VPN in 2026

- Your ISP can see every website you visit (in the US, Congress just voted to eliminate FCC ISP privacy rules)
- Public WiFi at coffee shops, hotels, and airports is a known attack vector
- Geo-restrictions on streaming content have gotten worse, not better
- After the FBI's router malware cleanup operation, it's clear: your network itself may not be trustworthy

**What a VPN does:** Encrypts all internet traffic between your device and the VPN server. Your ISP, your router attacker, and anyone on your WiFi network sees only encrypted gibberish.

## FAQ

### Do I really need all three?
For comprehensive protection, yes. Think of them as layers: the VPN encrypts your connection, antivirus catches file-based threats, and the password manager prevents credential compromise. They address different attack vectors.

### What's the most important tool?
If you can only afford one: **a password manager** ([AFFILIATE_LINK:Bitwarden] is free). Credential theft is responsible for 68% of breaches. Password managers eliminate credential theft entirely.

### Can one suite replace all three?
Some suites (Norton 360, NordVPN Ultimate) bundle VPN + AV + password manager. They're convenient but rarely best-in-class in all categories. Use the decision matrix above to choose.

### Are these tools enough to protect against the latest threats (Fast16, cPanel exploits)?
Consumer tools protect against commodity threats (phishing, credential theft, malware). State-level threats like Fast16 target infrastructure, not individuals. Your personal security stack won't stop a nation-state, but it will stop 99% of attacks you'll actually face.
